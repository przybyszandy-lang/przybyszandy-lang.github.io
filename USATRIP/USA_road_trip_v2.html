The Geographical Progression

Thirteen states, always onward:

New York → New Jersey → Pennsylvania → West Virginia → Ohio → Indiana → Illinois → Missouri → Kansas → Colorado → Utah → Arizona → California

West Virginia is just fourteen miles of I-70 through the Wheeling panhandle, the easiest state line on the route to miss.

Four things to book or buy before you fly

1. The one-way car hire. New York to Los Angeles carries a one-way drop-off fee that can run to several hundred dollars and varies hugely between companies. Compare several, collect at Newark Airport, and make sure unlimited mileage is included — this is a 3,000-mile hire.

2. The park pass. The Non-Resident Annual Pass ($250) covers entry for the car and waives the $100-per-person non-resident surcharge at Grand Canyon. Monument Valley is Navajo land and not covered.

3. New York tickets. Statue of Liberty pedestal or crown tickets, any Broadway show and observation decks sell out for weekend dates in April.

4. Grand Canyon and Moab hotels, and a Monument Valley tour. Grand Canyon in-park lodges sell out six to twelve months ahead; Moab is a small town with heavy spring demand.

The Journey02

The Shape of the Fortnight

Three Trips in One

A city, a continent and a canyon country. The route always moves forward — west across the country to Colorado National Monument, then south-west through the canyon country to the Grand Canyon and the coast — and nothing is driven twice.

Days 1 – 3

The city.

No car at all. Three days in Manhattan and Brooklyn on foot, by subway and by ferry. The car is collected on the morning you leave — parking in Manhattan costs more per night than many of the motels further west.

0 miles driving.

Days 4 – 8

Crossing the continent.

Five long days on the interstate: the Appalachians, the Ohio valley, the Mississippi, the Great Plains and finally the Rockies. One headline stop a day keeps it from becoming a blur.

Roughly 2,075 miles · 32 hours driving over five days.

Days 9 – 13

The canyon country.

Colorado National Monument and Arches, Monument Valley on the way south, two nights and a full day at the Grand Canyon, then Route 66 and a short last day to the coast.

Roughly 995 miles · 18 hours driving over five days.

How the Grand Canyon fits. From Moab the route turns south through Monument Valley and enters the Grand Canyon from the east, for two nights and a full day. To keep the last day under six hours, the night before LAX is in Kingman.

How to use the three days

The suggestion below divides the city geographically so that no day is spent crossing and recrossing Manhattan. Day 1 is shaped around whatever time you land — treat it as an evening, not a full day.

Getting around: the subway accepts contactless bank cards and phones at the turnstile (OMNY), with a weekly fare cap, so there is no need to buy a MetroCard. The Staten Island Ferry is free and passes close to the Statue of Liberty — a good fallback if Liberty Island tickets are sold out.

Places to Consider

Summit One Vanderbilt
The newest observation deck, beside Grand Central, with mirrored rooms and glass ledges. Worth considering as the alternative to Top of the Rock if you want the Empire State Building in the frame from much closer.
Grand Central Terminal
The 1913 concourse with its painted ceiling, the whispering gallery outside the Oyster Bar and the food hall below. Worth considering because it costs nothing and takes twenty minutes.
A Broadway show
Saturday evening is the obvious slot. Worth considering booking ahead for the big titles; the TKTS booth in Times Square sells same-day discounted seats for the rest.
Greenwich Village & Washington Square
Low-rise streets, the arch in Washington Square Park and the West Village’s small restaurants. Worth considering for Saturday evening if you would rather eat in a neighbourhood than in Midtown.
The Roosevelt Island Tramway
An aerial cable car over the East River, paid with the same contactless card as the subway. Worth considering as the cheapest high view of Midtown in the city.
Katz’s Delicatessen
The Lower East Side pastrami institution, open since 1888. Worth considering for lunch on the Saturday — expect a queue and a ticket system that confuses everyone.

Practical

Hotel: Midtown (around Grand Central or Bryant Park) is the most practical base — every subway line is within a short walk, and Penn Station for the Newark train is close.

Statue of Liberty: only Statue City Cruises tickets give access. Pedestal and crown tickets are limited and sell out weeks ahead.

Do not collect the car in Manhattan. A congestion charge applies to vehicles entering Manhattan below 60th Street, hotel parking is expensive, and the first hour of the drive would be the worst hour of the trip.

The day

Take NJ Transit from Penn Station to Newark Airport (about thirty minutes; change to the AirTrain) and collect the car there. You are on the interstate within minutes and never touch Manhattan traffic.

I-78 runs west through the New Jersey hills into Pennsylvania, past Allentown, to Harrisburg on the Susquehanna. From there the Pennsylvania Turnpike — America’s first long-distance motorway, opened in 1940 — climbs into the Appalachians and threads through ridge after ridge, including through several of the original tunnels bored under the mountains. Pittsburgh arrives suddenly: you come out of the Fort Pitt Tunnel straight onto a bridge with the whole downtown and its three rivers in front of you. It is one of the great arrivals in any American city.

Today is the gentlest of the five crossing days. Use it to get used to the car, the roads and the rhythm.

Tolls: the Pennsylvania Turnpike is entirely cashless. Tolls are billed through the hire company, usually with a daily admin fee — check what its toll package costs before you leave the desk.

Places to Consider

Hershey
The town chocolate built, ten minutes off the route near Harrisburg, with streetlights shaped like Hershey’s Kisses. Worth considering for the free Chocolate World ride and as the natural lunch stop.
Lancaster County & the Amish country
Farmland around Intercourse and Bird-in-Hand where horse-drawn buggies share the road. Worth considering if you leave Newark early — it is a detour of roughly an hour.
Gettysburg National Military Park
The 1863 battlefield, with a museum and cyclorama painting at the visitor centre. Worth considering instead of Hershey if history matters more than chocolate; about 40 minutes south of the Turnpike.
Flight 93 National Memorial
The field near Shanksville where United 93 came down on 11 September 2001, with a quiet memorial wall and the Tower of Voices. Worth considering as a sombre counterpart to the 9/11 Memorial two days earlier. Free; about 20 minutes off the Turnpike.
Ohiopyle State Park & Fallingwater
The Youghiogheny River gorge and waterfalls, with Frank Lloyd Wright’s Fallingwater house ten minutes away. Worth considering instead of Flight 93 — it is about 45 minutes south of the Turnpike, and Fallingwater tours must be booked.
Duquesne Incline, Pittsburgh
An 1877 funicular up Mount Washington to the best view of the city at night. Worth considering after dinner; it runs late and takes cash or card.

Practical

At the desk: confirm unlimited mileage, the one-way fee, the toll package and that the car may be taken through every state on the route.

Fuel: Turnpike service plazas are frequent and open 24 hours.

The day

South from Pittsburgh to pick up I-70, the road you will follow, on and off, for the next four days and well into Utah. It crosses the narrow panhandle of West Virginia at Wheeling, then the Ohio River, and settles into the long, flat, farmed country of Ohio and Indiana.

I-70 here shadows the National Road, the first federally funded highway in the United States, built westward from 1811. Old stone bridges, mileposts and inns survive alongside it, and US-40 — its modern number — is never more than a few minutes from the interstate.

The day’s set piece is Dayton, home of the Wright brothers, and the vast air museum on its edge. Plan around it.

Places to Consider

National Museum of the US Air Force, Dayton
The world’s largest military aviation museum: four hangars holding Air Force One aircraft, the Memphis Belle, a B-2 and a space shuttle trainer. Free, and ten minutes off I-70. Worth considering as the day’s main stop — allow two to three hours.
Wheeling Suspension Bridge
An 1849 bridge across the Ohio River, once the longest suspension span in the world, visible from I-70. Worth considering as a short stop to walk out and look at it.
German Village, Columbus
Brick streets and nineteenth-century cottages a few minutes from I-70 in the Ohio capital, with the Book Loft — thirty-two rooms of books. Worth considering as the lunch stop.
Dayton Aviation Heritage National Historical Park
The Wright brothers’ cycle shop and Huffman Prairie, the field where they learned to fly. Worth considering as a short add-on to the Air Force museum. Free.
Indianapolis Motor Speedway
Home of the Indy 500, with a museum and bus laps of the oval. Worth considering if you arrive before 5:00 PM; the race is in late May, so preparations will be under way.

The day

The longest day of the crossing, and the one that the clock helps most. You gain an hour at the Illinois line, so leaving Indianapolis at 8:00 AM puts you at the Mississippi at around 11:00 AM local time.

The Gateway Arch is 630 feet of stainless steel on the west bank of the river — the symbolic door to the West, and the right place to mark the halfway point of the crossing. The tram to the top runs inside the legs in small pods; the windows at the top look east over the river and west over the plains you are about to drive into.

From St Louis, I-70 runs across Missouri through rolling farmland to Kansas City, which arrives at around 5:00 PM. Kansas City is the barbecue capital of the country: dinner is the evening’s entertainment.

Put your watch back one hour at the Indiana–Illinois line. You are on Central Daylight Time from here until western Kansas.

Places to Consider

Gateway Arch tram
The four-minute ride to the top in a five-seat pod. Worth considering booking a timed ticket in advance — the museum underneath, on the westward expansion, is free and excellent. Allow two hours in all.
Chain of Rocks Bridge
A mile-long former Route 66 bridge across the Mississippi with a famous 22-degree bend in the middle, now for walkers only. Worth considering as a quieter view of the river, just north of the city.
Ted Drewes Frozen Custard, St Louis
A Route 66 roadside stand selling “concretes” so thick they are served upside down. Worth considering as the afternoon stop on the way out of town.
National WWI Museum and Memorial, Kansas City
The country’s official First World War museum, with a glass bridge over a field of 9,000 poppies and a tower with a view of the skyline. Worth considering the next morning before leaving; it opens at 9:00 AM.
Kansas City barbecue
Joe’s Kansas City (in a petrol station), Arthur Bryant’s and Q39 are the standards. Worth considering burnt ends, the city’s own dish.

The day

The day people warn you about — and usually the one they end up remembering. West of Topeka, I-70 crosses the Flint Hills, the last large stretch of unploughed tallgrass prairie in North America. In late April the ranchers burn the old grass, and the hills go from black to an almost luminous green within weeks.

Beyond Salina the land flattens, the trees disappear and the sky takes over. Grain elevators mark each town from fifteen miles away. The trick on the High Plains is to stop deliberately every ninety minutes; each of the places below is close to the interstate.

Goodland, the last town of any size before Colorado, is the overnight. It already runs on Mountain Time, which means an extra hour of evening.

Clocks back again. The Mountain Time line runs through western Kansas; Goodland is on Mountain Daylight Time, the same as Colorado and Utah. Tolls: the stretch of I-70 from Kansas City to Topeka is the Kansas Turnpike, which is cashless — billed through the hire company like Pennsylvania.

Places to Consider

Eisenhower Presidential Library, Abilene
The boyhood home, museum and grave of the Supreme Allied Commander, a few minutes off I-70. Worth considering as the first stop of the day.
Garden of Eden, Lucas
A Civil War veteran’s limestone cabin and concrete sculpture garden — one of the strangest folk-art sites in America. Worth considering as a 30-minute detour north of I-70; Lucas calls itself the grassroots art capital of Kansas.
Sternberg Museum of Natural History, Hays
Kansas was once an inland sea, and this museum’s centrepiece is a fossil of a fish that swallowed another fish whole. Worth considering as the lunch-time leg-stretch; it is right beside I-70.
Monument Rocks
Seventy-foot chalk pillars and arches standing alone on the prairie, about 25 miles south of I-70 near Oakley. Worth considering only in dry weather — the last stretch is gravel (see note in Practical Information).
Little Jerusalem Badlands State Park
Kansas’s newest state park: white chalk spires and a rim trail near Monument Rocks. Worth considering as an add-on to Monument Rocks on the same gravel roads.
The Van Gogh easel, Goodland
An 80-foot-tall reproduction of Sunflowers on an easel in the middle of town. Worth considering because you are sleeping a few minutes from it.

The day

The last crossing day, and by far the most spectacular. The plains rise so gradually that you will not notice you are climbing until, somewhere past Limon, the Front Range appears on the horizon as a white line.

Skirt Denver and climb straight into the mountains on I-70: through Idaho Springs and Georgetown, up to the Eisenhower Tunnel under the Continental Divide — the highest point on the US interstate system — and down past Vail and over Vail Pass. Then Glenwood Canyon: twelve miles where the interstate is cantilevered and stacked on two levels along the Colorado River between thousand-foot walls. It is one of the most remarkable pieces of road engineering in the world.

West of Glenwood Springs the mountains open out into desert mesas, and by Grand Junction you are on the edge of the canyon country. Colorado National Monument rises directly above the town.

If you are at Grand Junction by 5:30 PM, drive Rim Rock Drive this evening (see Day 9) — the canyon walls catch the last light and sunset is at 7:58 PM. Otherwise, it is the first thing tomorrow.

Check road conditions — mountain weather

Late-April snow on the Eisenhower Tunnel approach and Vail Pass is normal, and chain or traction laws can be activated for passenger cars. Glenwood Canyon also closes from time to time for rockfall. Check cotrip.org before leaving Goodland, and again at Denver.

If I-70 is closed through Glenwood Canyon, the signed detour is long; ask locally before committing.

Places to Consider

Red Rocks Park & Amphitheatre
A concert venue built between two 300-foot sandstone monoliths in the foothills west of Denver, open to walk around when there is no show. Worth considering as the gateway to the mountains — it is ten minutes off I-70.
Georgetown
A Victorian silver-mining town of painted wooden houses at 8,500 feet, right beside I-70. Worth considering for coffee and a first look at the high mountains.
Loveland Pass
The old road over the Continental Divide at 11,990 feet, an alternative to the Eisenhower Tunnel when it is open and dry. Worth considering for the view — but only in good weather, and it adds about twenty minutes.
Vail Village
The Tyrolean-styled ski resort, still in its closing weeks of the season in late April. Worth considering as the lunch stop.
Glenwood Hot Springs
A huge outdoor pool fed by hot springs, beside I-70 at the western end of the canyon. Worth considering as a reward after five days in the car — an hour here resets everything.

Practical

Altitude: you go from 3,700 feet at Goodland to over 11,000 feet in about four hours. Drink more water than feels necessary.

Brakes: use a lower gear on the long descents west of the tunnel and Vail Pass.

Fuel: fill in Denver; prices rise in the mountain towns.

The morning — Colorado National Monument

Rim Rock Drive runs 23 miles along the canyon rim between the Grand Junction and Fruita entrances, with more than a dozen overlooks: Cold Shivers Point, Ute Canyon, the Coke Ovens and Independence Monument. Staying in Fruita, the easy way is to drive in from the Grand Junction end and come out at Fruita, back onto I-70 westbound. Allow an hour and a half to two hours.

The drive — the back road into Moab

Rather than the usual route down US-191, leave I-70 at Cisco (exit 204) and take Utah State Route 128, the River Road. It is fully paved and follows the Colorado River for 45 miles through a red-walled canyon, past the Fisher Towers and Castle Valley, into Moab from the north. It is one of the best drives in Utah and almost nobody arriving from the east uses it.

The afternoon — Arches National Park

The park entrance is five minutes north of Moab. Over 2,000 natural stone arches in a compact area, with one paved scenic road of about 18 miles and most of the famous sights a short walk from it.

Timed-entry reservations. Arches required booked entry slots in recent springs, but the system was dropped for 2026. Check nps.gov/arch a month or two before travelling in case it returns for 2027. Either way, the car parks at Delicate Arch and Devils Garden fill by mid-morning; afternoon and evening are easier.

Places to Consider

Fisher Towers
Dark red, 900-foot spires of mud-coloured rock just off UT-128, with a short paved road to a picnic area and trailhead. Worth considering as the most dramatic stop on the River Road; even ten minutes is worth it.
Castle Valley
A green valley of farms beneath Castleton Tower, a free-standing 400-foot sandstone pillar. Worth considering for a photograph from the junction with UT-128 — no detour needed.
Dewey Bridge
The site of a 1916 suspension bridge, burned in 2008, where UT-128 meets the Colorado River. Worth considering as the first view of the river after leaving the interstate.
Delicate Arch at sunset
The defining Utah evening. Worth considering if you have the legs for it — start up the trail by 6:30 PM, carry a torch for the walk down, and expect company.
Moab’s Main Street
A small adventure town of outfitters, brewpubs and diners. Worth considering for dinner — Moab Brewery and Sunset Grill (on a hill above town) are the standards.

Practical

Fees: Colorado National Monument and Arches are both covered by the pass.

Water: carry at least a litre each on any walk in Arches; there is very little shade.

Weather: Moab is lower and warmer than Grand Junction — around 72°F / 22°C by day.

The day

The one long day of the canyon-country section, but it is long because it strings two of the most famous landscapes in America together. South from Moab on US-191 past the La Sal Mountains and Church Rock, through Monticello and Blanding to Bluff on the San Juan River, then US-163 through Mexican Hat — named after a rock — and across the state line into the Navajo Nation.

The approach from the north is the famous one: Forrest Gump Point, the long straight road with the Mittens and Merrick Butte rising at the end of it, comes before you reach the park, not after. Arrive around 11:00 AM, take a Navajo-guided tour (about 1½–2 hours) or drive the first viewpoints near the visitor centre, and have lunch on the terrace looking down the valley.

Then south-west through Kayenta and Tuba City, past the Painted Desert, to Cameron and SR-64. Enter the park at the East Entrance and stop at Desert View Watchtower for the first look at the Grand Canyon. Lipan Point and Grandview Point on Desert View Drive are both good sunset stops (7:09 PM) on the 25 miles to the Village.

Monument Valley — not ideal for a normal sedan

The 17-mile Valley Drive is rough dirt with deep sand in places; the Navajo Nation recommends an SUV or truck and hire contracts usually exclude unpaved roads. A guided tour solves this and opens areas closed to independent visitors. Monument Valley is Navajo land — the park pass is not accepted; entry is about $10 per person.

Clocks: Moab, Monument Valley and the Navajo Nation are on Mountain Daylight Time. Grand Canyon follows Arizona time, which does not use daylight saving — put your watch back one hour at the park. It stays the same as California from here to LAX.

Places to Consider

Wilson Arch
A large sandstone arch right beside US-191 south of Moab. Worth considering for a five-minute stop — you can scramble up into it.
Newspaper Rock
One of the largest panels of petroglyphs in the Southwest, spanning 2,000 years, a few minutes along UT-211 off US-191. Worth considering as a 25-minute detour; paved road, free.
Bluff Fort
A reconstructed 1880 pioneer settlement with free entry and a good café. Worth considering as a coffee stop before Monument Valley.
Goosenecks State Park
The San Juan River looping through 1,000-foot-deep entrenched meanders, a few minutes off US-163 near Mexican Hat. Worth considering for ten minutes; paved, small fee.
Navajo-guided tour, Monument Valley
The only way into Mystery Valley and the Big Hogan, and the comfortable way to see the Valley Drive. Worth considering booking ahead for 11:00–11:30 AM so the day keeps its shape.
Little Colorado River Gorge
A 1,200-foot slot gorge just off SR-64 before the park entrance; small Navajo fee. Worth considering as a ten-minute stop on the way to Desert View.

Practical

Fuel: Moab, then Blanding or Bluff, then Kayenta, then Cameron.

Book the Grand Canyon hotel now. In-park lodges sell out six to twelve months ahead for late April; Tusayan, just outside the South Entrance, is the fallback.

Places to Consider

Ooh Aah Point, South Kaibab Trail
The first viewpoint below the rim on the South Kaibab Trail, under a mile down, with a view along the ridge that gives the trail its fame. Worth considering as a shorter, steeper alternative to Bright Angel — reached by the orange shuttle.
Ranger programmes
Free talks on geology, condors and the canyon’s history at the main viewpoints, several times a day. Worth considering — the schedule is in the park newspaper and app.
Grand Canyon IMAX, Tusayan
A long-running film about the canyon and the river trips through it. Worth considering if the weather turns.
Helicopter flight from Tusayan
Flights of 25–45 minutes over the canyon from Grand Canyon Airport. Worth considering as the splurge of the trip; book ahead and expect cancellations in high wind.
Sunset at Hopi Point
The widest sunset view on Hermit Road. Worth considering for the final evening — shuttles run until about an hour after sunset.

The day

A whole morning still at the canyon — sunrise again, or the walk you did not get to yesterday — then lunch and an unhurried afternoon drive. This night in Kingman is what keeps the final day under six hours.

South through the forest to Williams, then I-40 west to Seligman, where you leave the interstate for the longest unbroken stretch of the original Route 66 still in use: 87 miles of two-lane road through ranch country, past Peach Springs and the Hualapai lands, Truxton and the old Hackberry General Store, into Kingman. It is slower than I-40, which is exactly the point on an afternoon with no deadline.

Tonight: fill the tank in Kingman (Arizona fuel is much cheaper than California’s) and repack for the flight — anything you need on board should be findable without unpacking the boot at LAX.

Places to Consider

Williams
A one-street Route 66 town of motels and neon, home of the Grand Canyon Railway. Worth considering as the lunch stop.
Seligman
The town that started the Route 66 revival, with Delgadillo’s Snow Cap drive-in and a barber’s shop turned visitor centre. Worth considering for half an hour.
Grand Canyon Caverns
A dry limestone cave 210 feet underground, reached by lift, beside Route 66 near Peach Springs. Worth considering as an hour’s stop that is cool on a hot afternoon.
Hackberry General Store
A 1930s petrol station crammed with Route 66 memorabilia and vintage cars. Worth considering because it is the picture everybody has of the old road.
Kingman Powerhouse & Route 66 Museum
A 1907 power station housing the Arizona Route 66 museum. Worth considering if you arrive before 4:00 PM.

Is the pass still worth it?

Yes. Without it, two people would pay $200 in surcharge at the Grand Canyon, plus about $90 in entrance fees at the Grand Canyon, Arches and Colorado National Monument — around $290 in all, against $250 for the pass. It still saves money, but only just.

Validity runs for twelve months from the month of purchase.

Planner’s Notes16

Notes

Planner’s Notes

The shape of the route

With the flight home fixed for 28 April, two nights at the Grand Canyon and a last day under six hours leave one night in Moab between Colorado National Monument and the canyon. The route therefore goes south from Moab through Monument Valley rather than west through Utah’s other parks.

If you want Bryce and Zion

They can be included in place of Arches and Monument Valley: Colorado National Monument → Capitol Reef and Torrey → Bryce → Zion, then one night (an evening and a morning) at the Grand Canyon before Kingman. Two nights at the canyon and Bryce and Zion together would need an extra day.

A word of caution

Five consecutive days averaging over six hours of driving is a lot, especially straight after a transatlantic flight and three busy days in New York. If that sounds too much, the alternative is to fly New York to Denver and start the drive there, gaining three days for the canyon country.

Accuracy

Distances and drive times are estimates. Use the Google Maps link on each day for live figures. Sunrise and sunset times are calculated for the dates and are accurate to within a few minutes.
